Global Setting
The world continues to be an unstable and unpredictable environment. Unrest in the Middle East has continued. The US and Iran have continued to trade strikes with no clear exit strategy for either side. The Strait of Hormuz is still largely closed, limiting oil movement and driving prices up. Civilian populations around the world are dealing with rising energy, petrol shortages and high inflation.
China has increased its presence in the South China Sea, taking advantage of the US’s focus into the Middle East. There is growing concern that China may choose to invade Taiwan.
Russia has continued to press into Ukraine, however, has made limited progress. Ukraine’s innovative use of drones, cyber warfare and insurgency has led to some embarrassing defeats for the Russian military.
The President of the United States has continued to push his ‘America First’ policy. European nations find his actions difficult to predict and relations have become increasingly fraught. He has repeatedly questioned the role of NATO and the value European nations are bringing to it.

UK Positioning
The UK PM is facing increasing pressure over his spend on supporting Ukraine and inability to secure regular oil supplies for UK homes. In the last month a number of damaging accusations and rumours have circulated regarding the PMs time at Oxford.
In response to increasing uncertainty on the world stage, the UK has been increasing it’s military readiness over the past 12 months, however, it is a slow process.

UK Intelligence Brief
Recent press and social media stories regarding the PM appear to be manufactured by international groups believed to be operating on behalf of the Russian government. This could be intended to undermine his authority and drive increasing discontent within the UK public.
Ukraine’s efforts have embarrassed the Russian president and he blames European countries, particularly the UK. He sees their intervention in Ukraine as an act of aggression. Russian cyber and military activity has increased considerably in the last 6 months. There have been regular cyber-attacks on critical UK infrastructure (power, rail & local government). To date, there has been no significant impact.
Russian incursions into UK airspace have increased, with Bear F (long-range maritime patrol and reconnaissance) aircraft being seen almost daily. A fleet of Russian warships is also exercising in the Atlantic ocean about 200 miles West of Scotland.
Whilst efforts have been made to increase our military readiness, it is a slow progress.
The UK equipment is generally of better quality than the Russian equivalents, however, is severely lacking in numbers. Numbers have been based on our ability to rely on NATO and Article 5 (an attack on one nation is an attack on us all). European defence has relied upon the addition of US forces, the stance of the US President is extremely worrying.
